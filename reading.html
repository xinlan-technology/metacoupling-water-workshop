Reading

Five papers on coupled human and natural systems, telecoupling, and metacoupling.

Selected papers

  • 01 Complexity of coupled human and natural systems

    Liu, J., et al. (2007). Science 317(5844): 1513–1516.

    Synthesizes six case studies from around the world, showing that interactions between human and natural systems vary across space, time, and organizational levels, with feedbacks, thresholds, time lags, and legacy effects.

  • 02 Framing sustainability in a telecoupled world

    Liu, J., et al. (2013). Ecology and Society 18(2): 26.

    Introduces telecoupling as a way to examine connections between distant places, organizing analysis around systems, flows, agents, causes, and effects.

  • 03 Systems integration for global sustainability

    Liu, J., et al. (2015). Science 347(6225): 1258832.

    Reviews systems-based approaches that integrate human and natural components across places, scales, and sectors, including how a solution in one place can create unintended consequences elsewhere.

  • 04 Integration across a metacoupled world

    Liu, J. (2017). Ecology and Society 22(4): 29.

    Extends the focus from distant connections to interactions within a system and between neighboring systems, bringing all three relationships into one framework.

  • 05 Leveraging the metacoupling framework for sustainability science and global sustainable development

    Liu, J. (2023). National Science Review 10(7): nwad090.

    Reviews uses of metacoupling in sustainability research and identifies directions for further work, including tracing flows, understanding cascading effects, and strengthening causal attribution.

These organizer-selected papers provide background for one lens examined in the workshop. The discussion will consider its value alongside existing hydrological concepts and methods. See Topics for the questions that guide this assessment.
