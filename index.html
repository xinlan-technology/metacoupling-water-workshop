Hydrology Beyond the Watershed

A Workshop on Metacoupling as a Lens for Hydrology

A small workshop bringing together researchers and practitioners to examine hydrological questions and water decisions that cross watershed boundaries, and to develop a research agenda.

  • Online
  • By invitation
  • January–April 2027, dates to be set with participants

Motivation

On a map, a watershed ends at its divides. Many of the forces that shape its water do not. Groundwater can flow beneath topographic divides, precipitation often originates as moisture evaporated outside the basin, canals and pipelines move water between basins, and trade and policy decisions made elsewhere can reshape local water use.

Hydrologists have studied many of these connections for decades. Less settled is when the watershed remains the right unit of analysis, and how to study connections across its boundaries alongside the processes within it.

One lens the workshop will examine is metacoupling, a framework for analyzing human–nature interactions within a system, between adjacent systems, and between distant systems.
