Hydrology Beyond the Watershed

A Workshop on Metacoupling as a Lens for Hydrology

A small workshop bringing together researchers and practitioners to examine hydrological questions and water decisions that cross watershed boundaries, and to develop a research agenda.

  • Online
  • By invitation
  • January–April 2027, dates to be set with participants

Motivation

On a map, a watershed ends at its divides. Many of the forces that shape its water do not. Groundwater can flow beneath topographic divides, precipitation often originates as moisture evaporated outside the basin, canals and pipelines move water between basins, and trade and policy decisions made elsewhere can reshape local water use. Hydrologists have studied many of these connections for decades. Less settled is when the watershed remains the right unit of analysis, and how to study connections across its boundaries alongside the processes within it. One lens the workshop will examine is metacoupling, a framework for analyzing human–nature interactions within a system, between adjacent systems, and between distant systems.

Questions

  1. 01

    When is the watershed not enough?

    Which hydrological questions, processes, and decisions cannot be adequately understood within a single watershed?

  2. 02

    How should we study connections across watershed boundaries?

    How can interactions within a watershed, between neighboring watersheds, and with distant regions be conceptualized, measured, and modeled?

  3. 03

    What is needed next?

    Which scientific questions, data, methods, and collaborations should be priorities for research on water connections across watershed boundaries?

Across all three, we will ask whether and where metacoupling adds analytical value, and where existing hydrological concepts and methods already suffice. Skeptical perspectives are as important to this discussion as supportive ones.

Plan

  1. Before the workshop: Gather perspectives

    Participants’ challenges, examples, conceptual gaps, and research needs, shared in advance of the online sessions.

    If you take part A short survey

  2. During the workshop: Discuss and debate

    Structured discussion of the three questions, from where the watershed falls short to what research should come next.

    If you take part One or two 2-hour online sessions

  3. After the workshop: Synthesize and refine

    Draft research priorities, prepared by the organizing team from the survey responses and the workshop discussions.

    If you take part Feedback on the draft priorities

  4. Outcome: Shape a research agenda

    A paper intended for submission to a leading interdisciplinary peer-reviewed journal in hydrology and water research.

    If you take part An invitation to coauthor

Background reading

Three papers that introduce telecoupling and metacoupling.

  • Framing sustainability in a telecoupled world

    Liu, J., et al. (2013). Ecology and Society 18(2): 26.

  • Integration across a metacoupled world

    Liu, J. (2017). Ecology and Society 22(4): 29.

  • Leveraging the metacoupling framework for sustainability science and global sustainable development

    Liu, J. (2023). National Science Review 10(7): nwad090.
